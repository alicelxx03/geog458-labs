1. Study area and dates:
I selected Puget Sound region near Seattle with the date of December 28, 2025 and the data is from AccessAIS of Marine Cadastre. I chose this area and date for the Vessel Traffic map because Puget Sound area in Washington has active vessel traffic, as it contains important waterways connected to the Paciic Ocean. December 28, 2025 was the most recent date available for the data, so I decided to use it to observe recent vessel traffic patterns. 

2. Interpretation:
I noticed that passenger vessels are the most active around Seattle downtown, traveling to and from Bainbridge Island, Harbor Isalnd, Bremerton, and West Seattle. There are multiple routes connecting these locations to Seattle downtown. Also, there are no vessels moving around midnight, when there is less human activity. For Cargo vessels, a few vessels travel back and forth between the northern part of Puget Sound and Tacoma, as well as between Seattle and the northern part of the region. Other vessels are widely spread across the Puget Sound region, with mamy clustered around Seattle downtown and Tacoma. 

3. Feature proposal for a potential user:
One potential user of my visualization is a transportation planner who wants to understand vessel traffic patterns in the Puget Sound region. After exploring Flowmap Blue, I would propose adding an origin-destination flow map to my visualization. FlowmapBlue is used to visualize various real-world phenamena with origin destination data. Adding this data would help identify major ferry terminals and ports with lines representing vessel movements between them by emphasizing flow destination, therefore, the transportation planners or users can easily find where the ferry is in and out, and make decisions for planning future ferry terminals and ports.
